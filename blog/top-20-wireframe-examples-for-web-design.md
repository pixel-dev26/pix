# Top 20 Wireframe Examples for Web Design

## **What a wireframe is actually for**

A wireframe is not a drawing. It is an argument about what a page is for, made cheap enough that you can afford to lose the argument.

I run Pixel Street, a web design studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We design and build for brands like Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico, and the wireframe stage is where those projects are quietly won or lost. Not because anybody drew the boxes well. Because a wireframe is the last moment at which a client can say _this page is trying to do three things at once_ without somebody having to throw away a week of finished visual design.

The twenty examples below are not here to be admired. They are a fidelity ladder, and the one decision this page is really about is how finished your wireframe should look.

### **Fidelity decides what gets argued about**

Show a client grey boxes and they talk about structure: what comes first, what is missing, why the enquiry form sits below the fold. Show the same client a polished screen with real type and one accent colour and they talk about the colour. Every time, on every project. I stopped treating that as a client failing years ago. It is a reasonable response to what you put in front of them.

Which gives a rule you can actually work to:

**Low fidelity** while the structure is still open. Grey boxes, no type choices, no brand colour. If it looks unfinished, people feel invited to change it, and that invitation is the entire point.

**Medium fidelity** once the structure is agreed and the question becomes hierarchy: what is large, what is small, what the eye lands on first.

**High fidelity** when you are testing behaviour rather than layout, which is where states, interactions and real data density belong. This is the stage that starts costing money, so arrive at it with the structure already settled.

The most expensive mistake I see is jumping straight to high fidelity because it demos better in a meeting. It does demo better. It also collects the wrong notes, and it collects them after you have paid to make everything look finished.

![Low fidelity keeps structure open and invites change, medium fidelity settles hierarchy once the structure is agreed, and high fidelity tests behaviour and real data density — the stage that starts costing money.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/diagrams/top-20-wireframe-examples-fidelity-ladder.svg)

### **Four things a wireframe earns you**

**It settles structure before anyone pays for pixels.** Layout and page order are decided while they are still free to change.

**It lets you test flow without aesthetics in the way.** You can walk a user through a journey and find the dead end, without anybody stopping to discuss the button radius.

**It gives non-designers something to point at.** Clients react far better to a concrete thing than they describe an abstract one, and a wireframe is the cheapest concrete thing I can hand them.

**It surfaces the problems that are expensive later.** A missing state, a form that needs a field nobody scoped, a navigation with no room for the section marketing forgot to mention.

## **Twenty wireframes, arranged by fidelity**

Read the ladder, not the individual pictures.

### **Simple wireframe sketch of a landing page**

![Pencil sketch on graph paper. A browser chrome is drawn at the top, then a "Creative Monkeys" logo block, a row of three crossed-out screenshot placeholders with titles and buttons beneath, and a second section splitting into an about column and a services grid. Margin notes in a second hand annotate the intent.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Simple-wireframe-sketch.jpg)

The cheapest possible version of the argument. No tool, no grid, no decisions you would defend. Its value is that anyone in the room can pick up a pen and redraw it, which is exactly what you want in a first meeting and exactly what you lose the moment the sketch looks official.

### **Detailed wireframe sketch across multiple landing pages**

![Pencil sketch headed "CommLogix v1" laying out four numbered page states on one sheet, with hand-lettered callouts naming a nine-item navigation, mini modules with teaser images, a modular nav that expands to full window width, and a fixed enclosed variant.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Detailed-wireframe-across-multiple-landing-pages.jpg)

Here the subject is the relationship between pages rather than any one page. This is where you find out that the navigation you agreed has no room for the section a stakeholder assumed was coming. Sketching several pages together costs an hour and regularly saves a fortnight.

### **Low-fidelity wireframe showing grid overlay**

![Grey block wireframe of a homepage with a pink column grid laid over it, showing how the logo, search field, five navigation items, hero image, four stacked banners, text blocks and a four-image row all align to the same underlying columns.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Lowfidelity-with-grid-overlay.jpg)

The grid is the contract between design and development, and this is the stage to sign it. Fix your column count and gutters here and every later argument about alignment has an answer. Skip it and you get a build where every section is spaced by feel.

### **Low-fidelity wireframe with annotations**

![Two search-overlay states drawn in outline, with numbered orange markers on the search field, result links and "view all results". Below, a questions box and a five-row notes table give each marker a name, an annotation describing the behaviour, and the user research it came from.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Low-fidelity-with-annotations.jpg)

On this one the annotations are the deliverable and the boxes are only somewhere to hang them. If a wireframe has to survive being emailed to somebody who was not in the room, it needs notes. An unannotated wireframe gets interpreted, and it never gets interpreted the way you meant.

### **Low-fidelity wireframe showing user flow for a music app**

![Three phone screens for a music app connected left to right by arrows: a song playing with transport controls, a song options menu, and an add-to-playlist screen. The arrows trace the route a user takes from playback to saving the track.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Lowfidelity-with-user-flow.jpg)

Screens laid out as a route rather than a set. Drawn this way, a dead end is obvious: a screen with an arrow going in and none coming out. That is a five-minute find at this fidelity and a support ticket at any other.

### **Low-fidelity example for a start-up company website**

![Two greyscale page mockups shown at an angle on a blue background. One is a short landing page with a hero, headline and three image blocks; the other is a longer page with an email capture field, a headline band, a three-image row and a footer with social icons.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Low-fidelity-for-startup.jpg)

Home, about, contact, and the path between them. Most early-stage sites need less than the founder thinks, and a wireframe at this level is the easiest way to have that conversation without it sounding like you are cutting scope.

### **Low-fidelity wireframe for a money-transfer banking app**

![A grid of eighteen phone screens on graph paper mapping a money-transfer app end to end: preloader, PIN entry, home, payments, summary, cards, history, and separate transfer flows by account number, phone number, recipient, contact and card number.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Low-fidelity-for-money-transfer.jpg)

Account fields, recipient selection, amount. What matters in anything moving money is not the happy path but everything around it: the confirmation step, the failed transfer, the wrong account number caught late. Wireframe the failures alongside the success or you will design them under deadline.

### **Low-fidelity wireframe showing user journey for a personal portfolio**

![Pale blue screens for a personal portfolio arranged at an angle, including a contact form marked "Contact v2", a work example page, an awards and vision column, and a project grid, with the contact form shown in both a full and a compact variant.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Low-fidelity-for-personal-portfolio.jpg)

Project showcase, skills, contact, room for writing. On a portfolio the order of the work is the design, and that decision is far easier to make in grey boxes than after you have spent an evening making the first project look beautiful.

### **Medium-fidelity wireframe for an eCommerce website**

![Greyscale product listing page for office chairs. A left sidebar carries filters for category, colour, availability and backrest height with counts beside each option; the main area is a grid of twelve product cards, one of which is hovered to reveal a photographic preview and a description panel.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Medium-fidelity-wireframe-for-an-eCommerce-website.jpg)

Browsing, search, product detail, cart, checkout. Medium fidelity is right for retail because the argument has moved on from what exists to what dominates. Which is bigger, the price or the delivery promise? You cannot settle that in grey boxes and you should not still be asking it in a finished comp.

### **Medium-fidelity wireframe for a sales management platform**

![Sales account dashboard. A radar chart plots productivity, growth, profitability, entrepreneurship, revenue and customer satisfaction against national and regional benchmarks; an open "chain of command" popover lists four named roles; below sit a donut chart of 185 accounts split into four segments and a dated follow-up list.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Medium-fidelity-wireframe-for-a-sales-management-platform.jpg)

Opportunity creation, account detail, progress tracking. Internal tools are judged on density rather than beauty, because somebody will look at this screen forty times a day. Wireframe it with the number of rows they will really have, not the four that fit neatly.

### **Medium fidelity wireframe for TV media content**

![Television interface in landscape. A voice search bar and a 19:45 clock sit at the top, a "Game of Thrones" episode row is highlighted with a play button, and a horizontally scrolling shelf of programme tiles runs beneath, each tile labelled with a title and genre.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Medium-fidelity-wireframe-for-TV-media-content.jpg)

A player, channel identity, supporting information. Television is navigated with a remote from across a room, so the thing to wireframe is the focus state: which element is selected, and where the selection goes on each direction press. Nothing else about the screen matters until that works.

### **Medium fidelity wireframe for a podline podcast app**

![Eleven phone screens for a podcast app called Podline, shown at an angle. They cover the for-you feed, trending quotes, top podcasts, a slide-out account menu, a profile with subscriber counts, and a now-playing screen with scrubber and transport controls.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Medium-fidelity-wireframe-for-a-podline-podcast-app.jpg)

Playback, episode detail, subscription management. The hard part of an audio app is that playback has to persist while the user wanders everywhere else, so a mini player belongs in the wireframe of every screen rather than on the one screen it lives on.

### **Medium fidelity wireframe for a decision-making app**

![Eight phone screens connected by labelled arrows showing how a question travels through a decision-making app: create a question, ask it personally, put it to a vote, request recommendations, add friends to the discussion, collect their suggestions, and open it publicly for anyone to answer.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Medium-fidelity-wireframe-for-a-decision-making-app.jpg)

Options, comparison, feedback on the choice. Anything that helps somebody decide lives or dies on how many options appear at once. Wireframing forces you to commit to a number early, which is uncomfortable and much better than discovering it in usability testing.

### **High-fidelity wireframes with charts and graphs**

![A wide board of linked high-fidelity screens for an analytics product, laid out as a flow with explanatory notes between them. It includes area charts, a world map, a summary row reading 58,000 and $103,000, a calendar picker, filter panels and a donut breakdown.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/High-fidelity-wireframes-with-charts-and-graphs.jpg)

Charts, graphs and tables together. High fidelity earns its cost here, because a data screen cannot be evaluated at low fidelity at all. A placeholder chart always looks readable. Load it with a real month of client data and half the layout stops working.

### **High-fidelity wireframe with multiple search options**

![Real estate site at near-final fidelity. The hero reads "Find Your Dream Home" over a search bar offering buy and sell modes with area and price selectors; behind and below sit an explore-listings grid with prices and locations, a testimonials video block, market statistics and featured posts.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/High-fidelity-wireframe-with-multiple-search-options.jpg)

Search by keyword, by location, by category. The screens worth wireframing on a search interface are the two nobody asks for: no results, and far too many. Those are where people leave, and they are almost never in the brief.

### **High-fidelity wireframe for mobile app analytics**

![Personal finance app screens in white and deep purple. They cover password and fingerprint sign-in, a dashboard with a masked card and a $1,655.77 balance, a transaction list with merchant names and amounts, a donut chart splitting income, spending and savings, a bar chart of statistics, and savings goals shown as percentages.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/High-fidelity-wireframe-for-a-mobile-app-analytics.jpg)

Dense data on a small screen, which is the hardest combination in this list. Wireframe it at real device width rather than on a comfortable desktop canvas, and decide early which single number the screen exists to deliver.

### **High-fidelity wireframe for a web app dashboard**

![Logistics dashboard for a truck app. A left rail lists dashboard, fleet, crash reports, statistics, reports, integrations and staff; the main panel shows a map of Europe dotted with vehicles, headline counts of 243, 71, 146 and 11, a pie chart of vehicle status, three ring gauges at 85, 72 and 79 per cent, and a table of the most profitable routes.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/High-fidelity-wireframe-for-a-web-app-dashboard.jpg)

Graphs, tables and controls on one canvas. The question a dashboard wireframe has to answer is what the user came to find out, phrased as a single sentence. If nobody on the project can say that sentence, the dashboard will end up as a wall of widgets and everyone will call it comprehensive.

### **High fidelity wireframe using one highlight color**

![Three phone screens for a hiking app, drawn entirely in grey with a single green accent used for the primary action on each: a profile with follower and leaderboard counts, a trail map with a floating locate button, and a welcome screen with Facebook, Instagram and email sign-up buttons.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/High-fidelity-wireframe-using-one-highlight-color.jpg)

Grey everywhere, with a single accent. This is my favourite constraint on the whole page. One colour means you have to decide what genuinely deserves attention, and it keeps the review honest, because a client looking at a mostly grey screen still talks about structure.

### **Hi-fi interactive wireframes**

![Several near-final page designs shown at an angle on a white background, separated by broad cyan, yellow and crimson diagonal bands. They include a pricing page with $25 and $50 tiers and a "Call Us" column, a hero reading "Your team's long-term memory", a dark client logo strip and a values page.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Hi-fi-interactive-wireframes.jpg)

Clickable rather than static. Once a wireframe can be used, you can put it in front of somebody and stay silent, which is the only reliable way to find out whether a flow works. Static wireframes get reviewed with a designer narrating over them, and the narration hides the problems.

### **Simple wireframe examples across a wide range of screen sizes**

![The same page shown at three widths in pale blue blocks. The desktop version places primary navigation beside the logo with a multi-column content grid; the tablet version stacks profile navigation above primary navigation and narrows to two columns; the phone version reduces to a single column with a "load more" control.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/11/Simple-wireframe-examples-across-a-wide-range-of-screen-sizes.jpg)

The same layout at several widths. Statcounter put mobile at 67.15% of Indian web traffic in June 2026, so a desktop-only wireframe is a wireframe of the minority case. Draw the narrow one first and the wide one second, and the hierarchy tends to come out right on both. There is more on that argument in our guide to [responsive web design](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/responsive-web-design/).

## **The tool matters less than the fidelity discipline**

I get asked which tool to wireframe in far more often than I get asked what fidelity to work at, and it is the less important question. Paper is a legitimate answer. Still, one name on the old lists has died and every price has moved, so here is July 2026.

**[Adobe XD is not a live option.](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)** Adobe said it had no plans to invest further, and XD is no longer sold as a single application to new customers. Any course still teaching it as a current tool is at least two years stale.

**[Figma](https://www.figma.com/pricing/) is the default.** Its Starter tier is free, and a full editor seat on the Professional plan is $16 a month. For most studios the free tier covers wireframing outright, because wireframing needs neither shared libraries nor branching.

**[Balsamiq](https://balsamiq.com/pricing/) is worth paying for precisely because it looks unfinished.** Its output is deliberately sketchy, which does the fidelity discipline for you and stops a client reacting to polish that is not there. It is $16 a month per editor on Starter, billed annually, with a fourteen-day trial.

**[Sketch](https://www.sketch.com/pricing/) remains Mac-only for editing**, at $12 per editor a month on yearly billing, or $120 once for a Mac licence with a year of updates. The browser app handles viewing and handoff, not design.

**[Axure](https://www.axure.com/buy) is the specialist choice**, at $29 per user a month billed annually, and it is worth it only if you need conditional logic and real variables in a prototype. For a marketing site it is a great deal of machinery I would never touch.

## **What I would actually do**

Sketch on paper until the structure stops changing. Move to grey boxes in whichever tool your team already owns. Add fidelity only when the question in the room changes, and never add it just because a presentation is coming. Wireframe the empty state, the error and the narrow screen, because those are where real projects break and they never make it into the brief.

And write on the wireframe what each page is supposed to achieve. A wireframe with no stated purpose is decoration, and it will be reviewed like decoration.
