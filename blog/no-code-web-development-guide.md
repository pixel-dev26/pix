# No-Code Web Development: What Survived Since 2023

**Summary.** No-code is excellent for internal tools, prototypes, single-purpose landing pages and small brochure sites. It is a poor bet for anything that has to be a durable, differentiated revenue channel. The reason is not that the software is bad. It is that you are renting a platform whose prices, plan names and free tiers change under you, and whose export options range from partial to nonexistent. Since the first version of this guide, entry prices rose, three of the named tools stopped existing, most free tiers were cut, and the industry moved from flat monthly fees to metered billing where your bill grows with your traffic. Budget for the meter, not the sticker.

I published this guide in 2023 and told you to look at Editor X, Integromat and Makerpad. All three are gone.

That is not a sentence I enjoy writing. It is also the most useful thing this page can tell you about no-code web development, so it goes at the top rather than in a quiet update note at the bottom.

I run Pixel Street, a web design studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We design and build for brands like Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico. No-code platforms are supposed to be the thing that makes a studio like mine unnecessary, and for a narrow band of work they genuinely are. So this is the honest version. What these tools cost right now, what their free tiers actually allow after several years of quiet tightening, which ones died, and the specific point where a no-code build stops being the cheap option.

Every price below was read off the vendor's own pricing page on 30 July 2026. They will move. That is rather the point.

## The short answer

No-code is excellent for internal tools, prototypes, single-purpose landing pages and small brochure sites. It is a poor bet for anything that has to be a durable, differentiated revenue channel. The reason is not that the software is bad. It is that you are renting a platform whose prices, plan names and free tiers change under you, and whose export options range from partial to nonexistent. Since the first version of this guide, entry prices rose, three of the named tools stopped existing, most free tiers were cut, and the industry moved from flat monthly fees to metered billing where your bill grows with your traffic. Budget for the meter, not the sticker.

## What the 2023 version of this guide got wrong

I am scoring my own homework here because a comparison post that never audits itself is worthless. Here is what happened to the tools this page recommended.

| Named in the 2023 guide | Status on 30 July 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Editor X, "the Wix alternative for professionals", at $22 to $75/month | Discontinued. Wix's [help centre](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-studio-faqs-migrating-from-editor-x-to-the-studio-editor) says Editor X completed the transition to Wix Studio in January 2025, existing sites were updated automatically, and it is no longer possible to create new Editor X sites. editorx.com now redirects to the Wix Studio page. |
| Integromat, recommended as the Zapier alternative | Gone under that name. integromat.com now redirects to a [shutdown notice](https://www.make.com/en/integromat-shutdown) on make.com. The product is Make, and its plan structure was rebuilt around credits. |
| Makerpad, listed as a leading no-code community | Gone. makerpad.co today serves an unrelated product about building AI companies that run themselves. The no-code learning community that name referred to is not there. |
| Thirteen dollar prices across Webflow, Wix, Squarespace, Bubble, Editor X and Shopify | Not one is still correct. Several plans no longer exist by name. |

Three dead names and thirteen wrong numbers in roughly three years. If you are deciding where to build something you intend to still own in 2029, that mortality rate is the finding. Everything below is written with it in mind.

Two things jump out of that table. The spread between the cheapest serious option and the most expensive is now roughly ten times, for tools that all describe themselves as no-code. And Glide, which most people still think of as a spreadsheet-to-app toy, has a first paid tier of $199 a month.

## The free tiers were quietly hollowed out

This is the change almost nobody writes about, and it is the one that will actually catch you.

The 2023 version of this page said, correctly at the time, that you could get a long way for nothing. That is much less true now. Read the current numbers again in one place:

- **Airtable** allows 1,000 records per base and 100 automation runs a month on free. A single moderately busy contact form eats that.
- **Zapier** allows 100 tasks a month, and only two-step Zaps. Any workflow with a filter and two destinations is a paid workflow.
- **Webflow** gives you two static pages and 1 GB of bandwidth on a webflow.io subdomain. That is a demo, not a site.
- **Adalo** and **Bubble** both let you build free and then charge you at the exact moment the thing becomes real, which is publishing.
- **Make** allows 1,000 credits a month and will not run a scenario more often than every fifteen minutes.

The pattern is consistent and deliberate. Free is now a trial with no clock on it. It is generous enough to make you build, and calibrated to stop working at roughly the point where you have sunk enough hours that leaving feels expensive. Softr and Carrd are the honourable exceptions on that list. Softr's free tier includes a custom domain, and Carrd charges $19 a year rather than $19 a month.

## The meter is the real price

The bigger structural change since 2023 is that no-code stopped selling flat monthly fees and started selling metered usage.

Bubble rebuilt its entire pricing around workload units, a measure of the database operations, workflows and web requests your app performs. Exceed your allowance and you either buy a workload tier or pay overages. Glide charges $5 per additional user and two cents per additional update on top of the subscription. Framer charges $20 a month per additional editor and meters AI features in credits. Webflow sells bandwidth in blocks, at $20 a month for 50 GB on top of your plan. Softr meters AI credits. Zapier meters tasks. Make meters credits.

What that means in practice is that your no-code bill is a function of your traffic. The successful version of your project costs more than the failed one, which is fine when you have priced it in and unpleasant when you have not. Adalo is the loud counterexample, marketing flat pricing with "no usage-based charges" as its differentiator, which tells you exactly how the rest of the category behaves.

![The monthly plan is the floor. Stacked on top of it are Bubble's workload units, extra editors at $20 a month on Framer and $5 a user on Glide, Webflow bandwidth at $20 per 50 GB, and metered tasks and credits on Zapier, Make and Softr, so the bill becomes a function of your traffic.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/diagrams/no-code-the-meter.svg)

So when you compare a no-code subscription against a [quote for a custom build](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/web-designing-cost-in-kolkata/), compare three years, not one month, and model the meter at the traffic you are actually hoping for.

## What this costs from India, which is different

Almost every no-code comparison you will read prices these tools in dollars for a buyer in California. That changes the answer here, and it changes it by a lot.

Two of these companies genuinely price for India. Shopify bills Indian merchants in rupees, from Rs 1,499 a month on Basic up to Rs 22,680 on Advanced, with POS Pro at Rs 7,000 per location. Squarespace also bills in rupees, at Rs 1,901 a month for Basic and Rs 4,310 for Advanced. Zapier lets you pick INR at checkout.

Webflow, Airtable, Bubble, Framer, Glide, Softr and Adalo do not. Webflow's page states plainly that all prices are in USD, per site, plus applicable taxes. For a Kolkata business that means the sticker price is a floor, and you add conversion and card charges on top of it, every month, forever, on an expense that never converts into an asset you own.

Run the arithmetic on the middle of the range. Bubble's Growth plan at $119 a month is a serious annual commitment in rupee terms for a small Indian business, and at the end of three years of it you own no code. That is the comparison worth making, and it is the same one I make in more detail in [Shopify versus WooCommerce for an Indian store](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/).

## Where no-code genuinely wins

Now the part where I argue against my own invoice, because pretending these tools are toys is how agencies lose trust.

**Internal tools.** This is where no-code is not just acceptable but clearly correct. An ops dashboard, an inventory tracker, an approvals queue, a client portal. Airtable with Softr on top will beat a custom build on time and cost, and nobody outside your company ever sees it, so brand distinctiveness is irrelevant. If you are paying a developer to build an internal CRUD app in 2026, stop.

**Prototypes and validation.** Testing whether anyone wants the thing before you build the thing. Bubble's free tier exists precisely for this and is good at it.

**Single-purpose pages.** One campaign, one event, one link-in-bio. Carrd at $19 a year with a custom domain is an absurdly good deal and I recommend it without qualification for that job.

**Small brochure sites.** A local business that needs to exist online with hours, services and a contact form. Wix or Squarespace will do this fine. You do not need a studio for it, and I would rather say so than sell you something you do not need.

The common thread is that no-code wins wherever the website is not the product, not the brand, and not the growth channel.

## Where it breaks, and what leaving costs

The failure mode is never the build. It is the exit.

Read the export terms before you fall in love with an editor, because they are worse than most people assume. Squarespace's own [help centre](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Exporting-your-site) is unusually blunt about this. Its export produces an .xml file aimed at WordPress, and style settings, custom CSS, product blocks, video blocks, portfolio pages and store pages do not come with it. The same page states that it is not possible to export content from one Squarespace site and import it into another. Leaving Squarespace is not a migration. It is a rebuild with your blog posts salvaged.

Webflow does offer code export, and then charges you for it. It sits on the Core Workspace plan at $19 a month, on top of your site plan. Exporting your own work is a subscription feature.

On the app builders the question does not really arise. A Bubble, Adalo, Glide or Softr application runs on the vendor's own infrastructure, which is why Bubble can meter your database operations in the first place. There is no folder of code to take with you. If the platform reprices, changes direction, or is acquired and wound down, the way Editor X and Integromat were, your options are to accept the new terms or start again.

Bubble, Adalo, Glide and Softr run on the vendor's infrastructure, so there is no code to take at all.](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/diagrams/no-code-what-leaving-costs.svg)

That risk is not hypothetical for this category, and I have the receipts three paragraphs into this very page. It is also the argument I make at more length in [AI website builders versus custom development](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/ai-website-builders-vs-custom-development/), which covers the same ownership question from the AI-builder side.

The honest framing is not "no-code is a trap". It is that you are choosing a lease, and leases are fine as long as you know you are signing one and you know the cost of moving out.

## Will no-code replace developers

Production, yes. Judgement, no. And I say that as someone whose studio sells both.

Assembling pages and wiring up CRUD screens is genuinely commoditised now. If a developer's entire value was turning a design into markup, that value has largely evaporated, and no amount of arguing about semantic HTML will bring it back.

What survives is everything upstream of the pixels. What are you selling, who is the competitor, who is the audience, what must this communicate, and where does the communication happen. No prompt and no drag-and-drop canvas answers those. When every business in your category can generate a competent website in an afternoon, the competent website is worth nothing, and the thinking behind it is worth more than it was. I have written about the same shift in [what AI is doing to design work](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/the-ai-revolution-in-the-design-sector/).

The useful version of the developer question is narrower anyway. Not "will no-code replace developers" but "can this specific project survive on a platform I do not control". Usually the answer is yes for a while and no eventually, and the skill is knowing which side of that line you are on.

## Where the community actually is now

The 2023 version of this page sent you to Makerpad, No-Code Founders and the no-code subreddit. One of those three no longer exists, which is a fair summary of how fast this space moves.

- **Makerpad** is gone. The domain now hosts an unrelated AI product. Do not go looking for the tutorials.
- **No Code Founders** is still running, as a free weekly newsletter and a tools and deals directory, and says it reaches over 38,000 founders a week. It has repositioned heavily towards AI tooling.
- **The r/nocode subreddit** is still active, and remains the least sponsored place to find out that a tool has quietly changed its pricing.

Vendor documentation is the other underrated source, and it is where I got most of the numbers in this post. Bubble's manual publishes its full price table and its workload rules openly. That is a good sign about a vendor. Wix's pricing page, by contrast, would not show me a stable figure at all, because prices and currency vary by location. Draw your own conclusion about which company expects you to comparison shop.

## How to choose, in three questions

Answer these honestly and the shortlist writes itself.

1. **Who sees this?** If the answer is your own team, use no-code, and use it today. Airtable and Softr, or Glide if you can justify $199 a month. If the answer is your market, keep reading.
2. **Is this a test or a channel?** A test should cost as little and take as short a time as possible, so use the free tier and accept the limits. A channel that has to produce leads and get cited by AI engines is an asset, and assets should be built on ground you control. That is the whole argument in [template versus custom web design](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/template-vs-custom-web-design/).
3. **What does year three cost?** Take the entry price, add the metered overages at the traffic you are actually targeting, add the rebuild you will pay for if the tool reprices or disappears, and compare that number against a one-off custom build. Do it in rupees if you are buying from India, because most of these vendors will bill you in dollars.

If you want the CMS side of this decision rather than the builder side, I compared the main options in [the guide to CMS platforms](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/best-cms-platforms/).

## FAQ

### Is no-code web development still worth it in 2026?

For internal tools, prototypes and small brochure sites, yes, and more so than in 2023 because the tools got better. For a website that has to differentiate a brand and generate revenue over years, the maths has got worse, not better. Entry prices rose, free tiers shrank, and billing moved to metered usage that scales with your success.

### What happened to Editor X?

Wix folded it into Wix Studio. Wix's help centre states the transition completed in January 2025, existing Editor X sites were updated automatically, and new Editor X sites can no longer be created. If you followed a 2023 recommendation into Editor X, you are on Wix Studio now whether you chose it or not.

### Is Integromat still available?

No. It is Make. The old integromat.com address redirects to a shutdown notice, and the product was rebuilt around a credit model. The free tier now allows 1,000 credits a month with a fifteen-minute minimum interval between runs.

### Which no-code platform has the most usable free tier?

Softr, on the current numbers. Its free plan includes 10 app users, 5,000 database records, 500 workflow actions and a custom domain, which is more than any other free tier here offers. Webflow, Adalo and Bubble free plans will not let you put a real site or app in front of the public at all.

### Do no-code tools cost more for buyers in India?

Effectively yes, for most of them. Shopify and Squarespace bill in rupees and Zapier offers INR at checkout. Webflow, Airtable, Bubble, Framer, Glide, Softr and Adalo bill in US dollars, so an Indian buyer pays the American price plus conversion and card charges, every month.

### Can I export my site if I leave a no-code platform?

Rarely in full. Squarespace exports an .xml file for WordPress that leaves behind your styles, custom CSS, store pages and portfolio pages, and cannot even be imported into another Squarespace site. Webflow offers code export but only on a paid Core Workspace plan at $19 a month. App builders like Bubble, Adalo and Glide run on the vendor's infrastructure, so there is nothing to export. Check this before you build, not after.

### Will these prices still be right next year?

Some of them will not. Every figure on this page was read from the vendor's own pricing page on 30 July 2026, and the last version of this guide had thirteen prices in it that all went stale within three years. Verify against the vendor before you buy.
