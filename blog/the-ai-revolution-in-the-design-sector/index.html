Khurshid Alam

June 8, 2023

I run Pixel Street, a design studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We design and build for Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico. In July 2026 I opened seventeen of the AI design tools that get recommended to studios like ours, one at a time, and checked every status and price against the vendor's own page.

Seven of the seventeen are gone. Two are Google products that no longer exist under the names they launched with. One was only ever a research page for a model that was never released to the public. Framer X, sold as design-to-code, has not opened a file since March 2023.

I am leaving that at the top because it is the most useful paragraph here. A tool list is the least durable thing anyone can publish about AI. What is durable is narrower and much less exciting: AI has taken over a specific, fairly small band of design work, that band has barely moved since 2023, and it does not include the part clients actually pay us for.

The short version

  • Of the seventeen tools checked below, ten are still worth opening. The rest are dead, renamed, or were never products.
  • The tools that survived are the ones attached to a business that sells something other than AI. Adobe, Canva, Figma and Midjourney are still here. The standalone AI-feature startups mostly are not.
  • AI is now genuinely good at three things in our work: generating filler and reference imagery, first-draft layout exploration, and turning a written brief into something a client can look at in an hour instead of a week.
  • It is still bad at the thing that decides whether a project succeeds, which is knowing what the brand is for and refusing the eleven directions that do not serve it.
  • Design headcount has not collapsed. In the 2026 survey I cite below, 10% of design leaders expected reductions and 28% expected to grow.

Seventeen AI design tools, checked in July 2026

Every status and price below came from the vendor's own page on 30 July 2026. Prices move; treat them as a starting point, not a quote.

One tool did not make it into either table. Looka, a logo generator, is still trading, but its site blocks automated access and I could not verify a single current price against its own pricing page. I am not going to print a figure I cannot stand behind. Separately, I would not point a business at a logo generator at all, for reasons I have written out in full in the logo design process. A logo is the output of a decision about positioning. Generating a thousand marks does not make that decision for you.

Midjourney is not Discord-only any more

Midjourney's reputation is that it operates inside a Discord server, with an interface most people describe as chaotic and overcrowded. That is out of date. Midjourney's own documentation now gives separate instructions for the web app and for Discord, and lists the web app first. If you have been avoiding Midjourney because you did not want to generate images inside a chat server full of strangers, that reason has expired.

Firefly has stopped being an Adobe model and started being a shopfront

Firefly's original pitch was commercial safety: Adobe trained it on licensed material so you could put the output in a client deliverable without a rights argument. That pitch still exists, but Firefly's current pricing page also bundles Google's Veo 3.1, Nano Banana Pro, FLUX.2, ChatGPT Image 2 and Runway Gen-4.5 alongside Adobe's own Image Model 4.

That matters if you sell to enterprise clients. "We used Firefly" no longer means "the output came from Adobe's licensed model". If your contract has an indemnity clause about training data, you now have to know which model inside Firefly produced the asset. Ask before you invoice.

Canva changed its plans, not just its features

Two things broke in the older advice. Canva Teams is no longer available to new sign-ups, replaced by Canva Business, which launched on 30 October 2025. And "Magic Studio", the umbrella name every roundup used, has quietly gone; the branding is now Canva AI, with heavier usage sold as a separate AI Pass add-on.

Canva prices by country, which is why the table shows rupees. Canva's India page lists Pro at INR 500 per month for one person and Business at INR 680 per person per month. For a Kolkata studio those are real numbers, not a rounding error off a dollar figure.

Runway moved house

If you have runwayml.com bookmarked it still works, but it is a permanent redirect to runway.com. The product is now split between a creative side and a developer side, and the flagship model is Gen-4.5. Standard is USD 15 a month, or USD 12 if you commit to a year.

What the dead tools had in common

Look down that second table and there is a pattern that is more useful than the list itself. Every single dead entry was one of two things.

The first kind was a feature pretending to be a company. Magician was a very good Figma plugin. Figma bought the team and the plugin evaporated, because a plugin whose value is one AI trick has no reason to exist once the host app ships the trick. Designify was the same story inside Canva.

The second kind was a model with a version number in its name. DALL·E 3 and Imagen were never going to be stable recommendations, because the vendors were always going to replace them and shut the old endpoints off. OpenAI gave developers roughly six months' notice. Google is giving Imagen 4 users until 17 August 2026.

So the practical rule I would now apply before adding any AI tool to a studio workflow is short. Does this company make money from something other than this AI feature? If not, expect to migrate within eighteen months, and do not build a client-facing process on it. That is the same argument I have made about AI website builders versus custom development, and it holds better here than it does there.

Where AI actually lands in a studio's process

Most writing on this subject takes a discipline at a time — graphic, product, UX, fashion, architecture — and ends up surveying industries the writer has never worked in. Here is the narrow version, limited to the parts of a studio's week I can actually account for.

Graphic and brand work

AI is now reliably faster than a human at resizing, cropping, background removal, masking and generating placeholder imagery for a layout nobody has approved yet. That is a genuine saving and it is not a small one, because those tasks used to eat junior time. It is not faster at deciding what the brand should look like, and the gap between those two statements is the whole business.

Interface and product design

Prompt-to-screen tools are good for the first twenty minutes and misleading after that. Figma Make will give you a plausible layout built from common patterns, which is exactly the problem: common patterns are what your competitors already have. I use this stage to find out what the obvious answer is, so that I can argue about whether the obvious answer is right.

Design to code

This is the category that quietly evaporated. Framer X and Sketch2React were the names to know, and neither is a going concern: Framer X has not opened a file since March 2023, and Sketch2React's site is still up but its copyright notice stops at 2023 and the last release I can find is from 2021. What replaced them is not a design-to-code tool at all. It is developers using AI coding assistants directly, which is a different workflow with different politics.

Research and copy

Synthesising interview notes, drafting interface copy, and writing the first version of a content model are the uses I would defend without hedging. They are text problems, and text is what these systems do best.

The techniques that actually survived

Four techniques were being sold as the near future of AI design in 2023. Two turned into ordinary working practice and two did not.

Generative design is now just how you explore variations, and it is unremarkable enough that nobody announces it. Natural language as the interface won completely; the reason the standalone AI plugins died is that you now type what you want in plain English into the app you were already using.

AR and VR did not become part of general design practice. The promise was that designers would use AI-driven tools to build immersive environments and gather client feedback inside them. For the kind of client work we do, that has not happened, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Deep learning and neural networks was never a trend in the first place. It was the underlying technology, listed alongside applications of itself.

What the 2026 numbers say, and what they do not

The best-documented recent evidence I could find is the AI in Design Report 2026, published in May 2026 by Designer Fund with Foundation Capital. It surveyed more than 900 designers across 60-plus countries in the first quarter of 2026, plus interviews.

Read the caveat before the numbers, because the report prints it itself: the sample "may skew towards respondents who have embraced AI more than the average designer, or who work at companies that are actively supporting adoption", and the authors present their findings "as directional rather than as absolute benchmarks". That is an unusually honest disclosure and it is the reason I trust the rest of it. It is also why you should read every figure below as the upper bound of adoption, not the average.

On headcount, which is the question every designer in Kolkata actually asks me: 28% of design leaders said they planned to grow their teams, 32% expected to hold headcount while increasing output, 10% expected reductions, and 21% did not know. Separately, 19% reported that headcount had already been cut while output expectations stayed flat or rose. Half reported no structural change at all.

On tools, the finding that surprised me was how thoroughly general-purpose assistants have displaced design-specific AI products. Claude was used by 78% of respondents and ChatGPT by 65%, with Perplexity falling from 34% to 13% year on year. 76% had used an AI coding assistant. Figma remained the most-used design tool. Nobody's answer was a bespoke AI design startup, which is consistent with the graveyard in my second table.

The number I would treat most carefully is the role one. Seven in ten leaders still named product designer as a core role on a product team, which means close to three in ten did not. That is not a headcount story yet. It is a story about what the job is called.

Where AI still fails, specifically

"AI lacks creativity" is the lazy version of this, and I have said it myself. It is not quite right. These systems produce plenty of novel output. What they do not do is refuse.

A generative tool asked for twenty logo directions will give you twenty. A designer worth hiring will give you three and tell you why the other seventeen were wrong for a company that sells to procurement departments in tier-two Indian cities. The value is in the elimination, and elimination requires knowing something about the client that is not in the prompt.

The second real failure is accountability. When a client's campaign underperforms, someone has to sit in the room and explain the decision. I lost two companies before Pixel Street, and the thing that survived both is that you cannot outsource the explanation. A model has no position to defend.

The third is data quality, which is the one everyone repeats and nobody acts on. If your brand has no documented positioning, AI will produce output that is confidently generic, because generic is the average of everything and the average is all it has.

Common questions

Is Midjourney still only available through Discord?

No. Midjourney's documentation covers both a web app and Discord, and puts the web app first. Plans start at USD 10 a month on Basic, with roughly a 20% discount for annual billing. There is no free tier.

Do I need to pay for AI tools to do professional design work in 2026?

You already are, indirectly. Figma's paid plans include a monthly AI credit allowance, and Adobe bundles Firefly into Creative Cloud. If you are asking whether you need a separate AI subscription on top, my answer is one at most. Pick the one attached to the app you spend the most hours inside.

Will AI replace designers?

Not on the evidence available. In the Designer Fund and Foundation Capital survey, 10% of design leaders expected headcount reductions against 28% expecting growth, and half reported no structural change. Remember that the sample skews towards AI-enthusiastic teams, which makes the low displacement figure more interesting, not less. What is changing is the job title. Design engineer and AI design specialist came up repeatedly as the roles leaders would hire instead.

What should a small studio or freelancer in India actually buy?

Start with the subscription you cannot avoid, which is your design app. Add Canva Pro at INR 500 a month if you produce social collateral in volume, because the alternative is doing it by hand. Add exactly one generative image tool if client work demands it, and expect to change which one within two years. Do not buy a tool because a roundup put it in a table. Including this one.

What I would tell a client who asks

AI has compressed the execution half of design and left the judgement half alone. That is why our timelines have shortened and our fees have not. The hours that vanished were the hours nobody wanted to pay for anyway.

The uncomfortable part, for someone who sells design: this makes the bottom of the market disappear. If your entire offer was producing artwork to a spec somebody else wrote, a Canva subscription now does that, and it does it for INR 500 a month. If your offer was deciding what the artwork should say, nothing has happened to you at all.

I would rather this page be short and correct than long and confident. So the last thing worth saying is procedural. I checked every tool in this post on 30 July 2026 and I will check them again, because on the evidence of the last three years about a third of them will be gone or renamed by 2028. If you find one that has died since I published this, that is my error to fix, not yours to work around. For the wider argument about which AI claims in web design held up, I keep a separate accounting in what survived the hype in AI web design, and a deeper walkthrough of prompting for layout work in using Midjourney in web design.
