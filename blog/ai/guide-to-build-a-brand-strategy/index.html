article

A brand strategy is a set of decisions, made once, in writing, so you are not making them again every Tuesday under deadline pressure. If the document you call a strategy rules nothing out — a customer you will not chase, a price you will not go below, a tone you will not use — it is not a strategy. It is a description of your company with better adjectives.

I run Pixel Street, a design and branding studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We design for Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico. Four years ago I made a cold call to ITC with a fancy deck and zero credibility, and before Pixel Street I lost two companies. Read what follows as an opinion formed at both ends of that table, not a neutral survey.

The short version

We keep three branding posts and they are deliberately not the same post. This one is the strategy document: who you are for, what you are against, where you sit against everyone selling the same thing. The engagement itself, stage by stage, is the branding process. Whether to break your category at all is the case for and against a disruptive brand, where my answer is usually no.

A finished strategy answers four questions somebody could disagree with: what you sell, why you exist, how you behave, and who loses when you win.

Defining Brand Identity (Using the What-Why-How Model)

What (Product/Service)

Start with what you actually sell, plainly enough that a stranger could repeat it back. For a skincare company, organic cruelty-free face creams and serums. The temptation is to leap to the promise and skip the boring inventory. Do the inventory first. A promise not anchored to something you sell collapses the moment a customer asks what they are buying.

Why (Purpose, Vision, Mission)

The why answers what the brand exists for. For our skincare brand: helping people feel comfortable in their own skin with products that do not cost the planet anything.

One test before you write it down. Could a direct competitor put their logo on your purpose without anyone noticing? Then it is doing no work. Most purpose statements fail this, including the ones framed on the walls of very large companies.

How (Values and Processes)

Your how is the values you hold and the processes that make them true. Transparency, sustainability, ethical sourcing: each has to cash out as something operational or it is decoration. Transparency means publishing the supplier list. A value nobody has to spend money to honour is not a value.

Understanding Your Audience (Incorporating Elements from the Brand Key Model)

Consumer Insights

Understand your audience specifically, not demographically. Surveys, social listening and direct feedback all pay better when you hunt for contradictions instead of confirmations. You might find customers who say they value sustainability and still buy on visible results in two weeks. That tension is the finding. The comfortable version, where they want exactly what you already sell, usually means you asked leading questions.

Brand Essence and Attributes

Brand essence is the emotional core rather than the functional one — for the skincare brand, natural confidence. Attributes support it: pure ingredients, visible results. Keep the list short. A brand with nine attributes has none; nobody outside the company remembers more than two.

Brand Positioning

Developing a Unique Position (Utilizing the Brand Key Model)

[IMG: branding strategy brand key model]

Source: Gust de Backer

Unique Selling Proposition:

Your USP is the thing you can say that a competitor cannot say back. That clause is the entire test, and it is why most stated USPs are not ones. Quality, service and customer focus get claimed by everyone, which makes them table stakes. A rare organic blend nobody else can source is a USP. Caring about your customers is a minimum standard you should be embarrassed to advertise.

Positioning Statement:

For eco-conscious buyers who want visible results, [Brand Name] makes organic skincare that works fast enough to keep using. Useful format, and half a position, because it describes a customer without naming an opponent.

The competitive frame, which is the part most decks skip

Positioning is comparative or it is nothing. People buy you instead of something specific, and that something is often not a competitor at all. It is doing nothing, or the bottle already in the bathroom cabinet.

Write the frame as three lines and make them uncomfortable:

  • Instead of what. Name the real alternative, including the two everyone omits: doing nothing, and the incumbent the customer is already used to.
  • Better on which single axis. One. Claim three and you have claimed none, because the reader keeps whichever they already believed.
  • Worse on what. If you cannot name the trade you are asking a customer to accept, you have not chosen a position. You have written a wish.

The third line is the one clients argue about and the one that does the work. Every real position costs something.

[IMG: A position is three lines: instead of what, naming the real alternative including doing nothing and the incumbent the customer already uses; better on which single axis, one only; and worse on what, naming the trade you are asking a customer to accept. The third line is the one that does the work.]

Visual and Verbal Identity

Strategy decides, identity expresses. A logo commissioned before the position is settled is a guess you pay for twice.

At this stage you are not choosing colours. You are writing the constraints that make the choice arguable later: the personality the identity must carry, the competitors it must not resemble, the contexts it has to survive — a shopfront in Salt Lake, a phone screen, a printed carton, a favicon. How logo, colour and typography actually get produced sits in the branding process; the logo stage is the logo design process. The one verbal decision that belongs here is brand voice, because it is a constraint rather than an execution. Write it as pairs — warm but not chatty, expert but not lecturing. A single adjective gives a copywriter nothing to push against.

Marketing and Communication Strategy

Channel choice is downstream of the position, and treating it as the strategy is the most common way a branding project goes wrong. Instagram is not a strategy. Being the brand eco-conscious buyers trust for fast results is, and it tells you Instagram matters and print probably does not. What belongs in the document is the rule, not the calendar: channels chosen, channels declined, and the one message all of them reinforce. If a new platform launching forces you to rewrite your positioning, it was never holding anything up.

Measurement and Evolution

Evaluating Brand Performance

Key Performance Metrics:

Decide what you are measuring before the work starts. Choose afterwards and you will choose whichever number moved, which is not measurement, it is a press release. Prompted and unprompted recall, retention, and the share of enquiries arriving already knowing what you do are the honest ones. Analytics and follower counts are easy to collect and mostly report traffic.

Be patient with the clock. Positioning shows up in what people say about you unprompted, and that lags by quarters. Anyone selling a thirty-day proof point is selling a campaign.

Feedback and Adaptation:

Separate the two things that get confused here. Adjusting how you express a position is maintenance. Changing the position itself is a rebrand with real costs, and the rebranding roadmap covers when that is justified.

Final Thoughts

The document is finished when it can settle an argument you have not had yet. Most of what gets presented to me as brand strategy reads well and decides nothing, so the next hard call is made on instinct anyway and the deck goes in a drawer.

Test yours before signing it off. Name the customer you are turning away, the axis you are worse on, and the alternative your buyer is really choosing between. Fill in all three and still believe it, and you have a strategy. Leave them blank and you have a very handsome description.

If you would rather argue it out with someone than fill in a template, that is the work we do at Pixel Street in Kolkata.
