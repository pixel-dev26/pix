How to actually choose between them

The standard advice in this genre is to check experience, read reviews, get a free quote, compare prices, check the portfolio and ask for references. That is six items, it is really two, and one of the two is close to useless. Here is what I would actually do, having sat on the other side of this conversation for years.

Ask to see the app in the store, not in a case study

A case study is a designed artefact. A store listing is not. Ask for the link, check who the developer of record is, read the recent one-star reviews, and look at the last update date. An app that has not shipped an update in two years tells you how that relationship ended.

Ask who owns the code and the accounts

This separates a supplier from a landlord, and almost nobody asks it before signing. Whose Apple Developer account will your app live in? Whose Play Console? Who holds the repository, the signing keys, the push certificates? If the answer is the agency, you do not own your app. You are renting it, and the rent gets renegotiated the day you try to leave. I lost two companies before Pixel Street, and the lesson that survived both is that you never build your main asset on ground somebody else controls.

Treat the free quote as the least informative thing you get

A quote produced before anybody has scoped your data model is a sales document, not an estimate, and comparing several of them mostly tells you who is most willing to guess low. For why the numbers in this city vary so wildly, see what web design costs in Kolkata. The same logic applies to apps.

Decide first whether you need an agency at all

For a single well-defined app with a fixed scope, a good freelancer can be the better answer; for anything needing sustained maintenance an agency usually is. Freelancer versus agency covers that trade-off properly, and it is worth settling before you start taking meetings.
