# PR Companies in Kolkata: 4 That Actually Do PR

**Summary.** All six companies are trading. Only four of them still sell public relations.

A disclosure before anything else. I run Pixel Street, a web design and branding studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We do not sell public relations, but we sell branding and digital marketing to the same businesses these agencies pitch, from the same few square kilometres. A roundup of local agencies published on a local agency's own blog is not a neutral directory, and pretending otherwise would be the least credible thing on the page.

So there is no ranking here. I have no basis for telling you which of these teams gets better coverage, and neither does any other list on this subject, because the claims those lists repeat are copied straight off the agencies' own marketing pages. What I can check is the thing that wastes your afternoon: whether these firms still exist, and whether they still do public relations at all.

On **30 July 2026** I opened every one of their websites. Two of the six are no longer PR agencies.

![Public Relations company](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/10/pr.jpeg)

## The short answer

All six companies are trading. Only four of them still sell public relations.

**Speaking Mirror** has repositioned as a digital marketing and content agency, and **WYSIWYG** describes itself on its own about page as a graphic design studio. Neither uses the words public relations, media relations or press release anywhere on its site. Both are healthy businesses. Neither is the answer to the question this page is supposed to answer, so neither is listed below, and I have set out exactly what I found.

I have not padded the list back up either. Four agencies I actually opened are worth more than six with two wrong answers among them, and a round number in a heading was never worth anything to you.

## Two agencies that no longer do PR

### Speaking Mirror is now a digital marketing agency

![Speaking Mirror website image](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/10/Speaking-mirror.jpg)

Speaking Mirror is the most interesting technical case on this page, and it is worth a paragraph because it shows why a link checker is not enough. Request `speakingmirror.in` and you get a clean 200 response containing about 4 KB: a page title and an empty container. Every path on the site returns the same bytes. A crude checker calls that healthy. A sceptical one calls it a parked page. Both are wrong. It is a JavaScript application that assembles itself in the browser, and the real site lives inside a half-megabyte script file.

Read that file and you find a completely current agency: a 2026 copyright line, three offices, and a capability list of social media, content creation, performance marketing, brand strategy, influencer marketing and branding. Its own meta description calls it a "Full-scale marketing agency specializing in Digital Marketing & Content Creation". Public relations appears nowhere in it. Neither does media relations, nor press release, nor anything adjacent.

It has also moved. The Golpark address is long gone, the Kolkata office is now on Shakespeare Sarani, and there are Mumbai and Dubai offices as well. It is a good agency doing something else. It is listed, correctly, in our [Kolkata social media marketing agencies](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/best-social-media-marketing-companies-in-kolkata/) roundup instead.

### WYSIWYG is a graphic design studio

![WYSIWYG website photo](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/media/2022/10/wysiwyg.png)

WYSIWYG Communications sits behind a JavaScript challenge that returns 403 to every automated request, including for its robots file and sitemap, so no research tool and no language model can read it. I opened it in an actual browser instead, which is the only way anyone is seeing this site.

Behind the wall is a thoroughly alive firm: copyright 2026, founded in 1992, a portfolio running from ITC Hotels to Ambuja Neotia, and a piece in T2 Online dated 10 May 2026 about its move to a new studio. Its about page opens by saying that for more than 32 years it has been "a graphic design studio", and lists its strengths as packaging design, identity creation, web design, visual merchandising, environmental graphics and publicity material for events. Its published team is graphic designers, an illustrator, a copywriter and pre-production staff.

Public relations and digital marketing appear nowhere on its site, and events appear only as design work produced for other people's events. It is a graphic design studio. If it is on your PR shortlist, that is the wrong shortlist for it — and if what you need is identity or packaging, it is a serious place to start.

### Why there are no client names or awards on this page

PR agency sites in this city carry blue-chip client logos, client counts in the hundreds, awards, and round-number decades of experience. I could confirm almost none of those relationships from a primary source. Where an agency's own portfolio does name the client, as WYSIWYG's does for ITC Hotels, that is still the agency's word for it, and a logo on a portfolio page can mean a five-year retainer or one afternoon of subcontracted work.

That distinction matters more in public relations than almost anywhere else, because the entire product is somebody else's credibility. An agency that borrows a client's name to win yours has told you something about how it treats a client's name.

## How to choose one, from somebody who buys this service

The standard advice on this subject is that a good PR agency understands your business, is creative, builds relationships, responds fast and handles a crisis. Nobody has ever chosen an agency on that basis, because no agency has ever claimed the opposite. Here is what I would actually ask.

### Ask which journalists they have placed in the last ninety days

Not which publications. Which people, and what the story was. Public relations is a relationships business, and relationships belong to individuals, not to logos on a website. An agency that can name recent placements and the reporters behind them is describing something real. An agency that answers with a media list is describing a spreadsheet you could buy.

### Ask what they would refuse to pitch

A firm that will place anything you hand it has no editorial judgement, and editorial judgement is the entire value. The useful answer names a story they killed and why. It will be slightly awkward to hear, which is how you know it is true.

### Settle who owns the relationship before there is a crisis

The worst possible time to discover that your agency's key contact has left, or that the journalist relationship belonged to a freelancer they subcontract, is the week you need it. Ask who specifically will hold your account, whether they are staff, and what happens to your coverage if that person resigns. I lost two companies before Pixel Street, and the lesson that survived both is never to build your main asset on ground somebody else controls. A press relationship you cannot name is exactly that.

### Agree what counts as a result before you sign

Coverage volume is the metric agencies volunteer, because it is the one they can always deliver. Decide instead what the coverage is for, whether that is inbound enquiries, investor confidence, recruitment or defending a reputation you already have, and write that into the agreement. The rest of what separates a good agency engagement from a bad one is not specific to PR at all, and we wrote it up in [the questions worth asking before you hire an agency](https://pixelstreet.in/blog/questions-before-hiring-web-design-agency/).
