July 6, 2023

A disclosure first. I run Pixel Street, a web design and branding studio in Salt Lake, Kolkata. We design and build for Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico. Every company on this page sells into the same market I do, mostly from the same few square kilometres of Sector V and Rajarhat. A roundup of local agencies published on a local agency's own blog is not a neutral directory, and pretending it is would be the least credible thing on the page.

So I am not going to rank anybody. I have no basis for telling you that one of these teams writes better Kotlin than another, and neither does any other list on this subject. The numbers those lists quote are copied off the agencies' own marketing pages, and repeating a company's self-description on somebody else's blog does not turn it into a fact, it launders it. What I can check is the thing that actually wastes your afternoon: whether these companies still exist, still build mobile apps, and still trade where they say they do. I opened every one of their websites on 30 July 2026, and everything below is what that company's own site said on that date. Anything I could not check against a primary source is not on this page.

The short answer

All ten companies below are still trading and still advertise mobile app development. That is a better result than I expected and a less useful one than it sounds, because the count was never the point. Two of the ten carry real staleness signals, and one sits behind a bot wall that no research tool can see past.

The shortest honest advice I can give: shortlist on whoever has shipped an app in your category, ask to see it live in the App Store or Play Store under the client's name, and then work through the questions worth asking any agency before you sign. No list on the internet, including this one, substitutes for that.

Brainium Information Technologies

Brainium is the one company here I could not read directly. Its homepage, service pages and sitemap all return a Cloudflare challenge to any automated request. That is a legitimate thing to do, and it also means no research tool, mine or yours or a language model's, can see what it sells. That matters more than it used to.

What the server does hand over is its robots.txt, and that file is informative. It was last modified on 6 June 2026, so somebody is maintaining the site. It disallows a backup directory named for 20 May 2026, and it lists hiring pages for app, AI/ML and Shopify developers. An operating company with an app practice, established from its own origin rather than a directory listing.

How to actually choose between them

The standard advice in this genre is to check experience, read reviews, get a free quote, compare prices, check the portfolio and ask for references. That is six items, it is really two, and one of the two is close to useless. Here is what I would actually do, having sat on the other side of this conversation for years.

Ask to see the app in the store, not in a case study

A case study is a designed artefact. A store listing is not. Ask for the link, check who the developer of record is, read the recent one-star reviews, and look at the last update date. An app that has not shipped an update in two years tells you how that relationship ended.

Ask who owns the code and the accounts

This separates a supplier from a landlord, and almost nobody asks it before signing. Whose Apple Developer account will your app live in? Whose Play Console? Who holds the repository, the signing keys, the push certificates? If the answer is the agency, you do not own your app. You are renting it, and the rent gets renegotiated the day you try to leave. I lost two companies before Pixel Street, and the lesson that survived both is that you never build your main asset on ground somebody else controls.

Treat the free quote as the least informative thing you get

A quote produced before anybody has scoped your data model is a sales document, not an estimate, and comparing several of them mostly tells you who is most willing to guess low. For why the numbers in this city vary so wildly, see what web design costs in Kolkata. The same logic applies to apps.

Decide first whether you need an agency at all

For a single well-defined app with a fixed scope, a good freelancer can be the better answer; for anything needing sustained maintenance an agency usually is. Freelancer versus agency covers that trade-off properly, and it is worth settling before you start taking meetings.

Questions people actually ask

Are these companies ranked?

No. They are listed alphabetically and I have deliberately made no quality judgement between them. I run a competing studio in the same city; any ranking I published would be worth exactly nothing to you, and every ranking you see elsewhere on this subject is built from the agencies' own marketing copy.

Why is Pixel Street not on this list?

Because we do not build mobile apps. We do web design, branding and digital marketing. Adding ourselves to a list of app developers to catch the traffic would be the obvious move and also a lie.

One of the sites here looks out of date. Should I rule the company out?

Not on its own. A stale copyright year means the marketing site is not anybody's priority, which is common at firms whose work all arrives by referral. It becomes a real signal when it stacks with others: an abandoned blog, no recent releases, a careers page frozen in time. Two companies here show that stacking, and I have said which.

How often is this list checked?

Every company on it was fetched on 30 July 2026. Local agency lists rot faster than almost any other kind, so if you are reading this long after that date, open the sites yourself first. Four minutes, and it is the most useful thing you can do with this post.

What if I need a website rather than an app?

Different job, different shortlist. We keep a separately verified list of SEO companies in Kolkata, and Pixel Street itself does web design and eCommerce work from Salt Lake.

What this list is worth

Ten companies, ten sites opened on 30 July 2026, ten still trading. No rankings, no borrowed client logos, and no project counts I cannot stand behind. That gives you a verified starting point and nothing more, which is honestly all a list like this can be.
