Khurshid Alam

July 5, 2023

I run a branding and web design studio in Kolkata. We design for Coca-Cola, ITC and Marico, and every few months somebody on the team asks me for a reading list. That is why this page exists.

On 30 July 2026 I checked all twelve against their publishers. Six of them have a newer edition than the one a typical reading list points at. Two are no longer buyable on Amazon India at all. A roundup of famous branding books is the easiest article on the internet to write and the easiest to get quietly wrong, because nobody ever returns to it.

So this list is built around what I can actually prove: what each book contains, which edition is current, and whether you can still buy it in India. I am not going to tell you which of these rearranged my thinking, because at that point I would be performing rather than helping. Opinions about branding are free. A correct ISBN is not.

One housekeeping note, since book roundups usually hide this. None of the Amazon links on this page carries an affiliate tag. I checked every query string. We earn nothing whether you buy or not.

Which edition to buy in 2026

Editions matter more in a marketing book than in a novel. A branding book written before generative search arrived, or one whose author has since died and whose latest edition was finished by somebody else, is not wrong. You just need to know which one you are holding. Here is the state of all twelve on 30 July 2026, checked against the publisher or the author's own site rather than against another blog post.

Book and author Edition to buy in 2026 What it is for
Start with Why - Simon Sinek 15th Anniversary Edition, Penguin, 15 May 2025, with a new foreword. The 2011 Portfolio paperback most lists still cite is still sold. Purpose and internal alignment. Least practical book here, most quoted.
The Brand Gap - Marty Neumeier Revised edition, New Riders, 25 August 2005. The 2003 first edition is unavailable on Amazon India. The two-hour introduction. Start here if you have never read a branding book.
The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding - Al Ries and Laura Ries HarperBusiness paperback, 17 September 2002; the Kindle edition linked below is dated 6 October 2009. Positioning, stated as rules. Al Ries died in 2022, so this is a closed body of work.
Personal Branding For Dummies - Susan Chritton 2nd edition, Wiley, 14 July 2014. The 2012 first edition is the one usually cited. Structured self-presentation for people who want a checklist.
The Power of Visual Storytelling - Ekaterina Walter and Jessica Gioglio McGraw-Hill, 3 March 2014. No newer edition. Visual content on social platforms. The most dated book on the list.
The Brand You50 - Tom Peters Knopf, 21 September 1999. No newer edition. A provocation about treating your own career as a brand.
Building a StoryBrand - Donald Miller Building a StoryBrand 2.0, HarperCollins Leadership, 7 January 2025. The 2017 edition is superseded. Messaging. The most directly usable book here for a small business.
Designing Brand Identity - Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson 6th edition, Wiley, 2024, retitled A Comprehensive Guide to the World of Brands and Branding. Most lists still cite the 2017 5th edition. The reference manual. The one book on this list a whole team can work from.
Sticky Branding - Jeremy Miller Dundurn, 10 January 2015. No buyable Amazon India listing as of 30 July 2026. Small and mid-size B2B firms trying to stand out in a commodity category.
Identity Designed - David Airey Rockport, 2019. A companion volume, Identity Designed: The Process, followed in 2024. Case studies of real identity projects, start to finish.
Building Strong Brands - David A. Aaker Free Press, 1 December 1995. No newer edition. The academic end. Brand equity as a measurable asset.
Logo Design Love - David Airey 3rd edition, New Riders, 2026, retitled A Guide to Creating Iconic Brand Marks. It supersedes the 2014 2nd edition, which is the one most lists still cite. Logo craft specifically, not brand strategy.

Two of these entries need saying out loud rather than leaving in a table. Al Ries died on 7 October 2022, aged 95. Alina Wheeler died in 2023, and the sixth edition of Designing Brand Identity was carried forward by Rob Meyerson. Both books are still worth your money. But a recommendation written in the present tense about a living author reads differently once you know, and I would rather you heard it here than found out afterwards.

Different types of Branding Strategies!

The reason a single list of twelve books is a poor answer is that "branding" covers at least seven different jobs, and no book covers more than two of them well. Read the type of branding you are actually being paid to do.

Personal Branding:

Personal branding is the practice of marketing a person and their career as a brand: building a distinct identity, promoting it through chosen channels, and managing its reputation over time. On this list, Personal Branding For Dummies and The Brand You50 are the two books aimed squarely at it, and they could not be less alike. Chritton gives you a workbook. Peters gives you a shove.

Product Branding:

Product branding creates an identity for one product or service rather than for the company behind it: the name, the mark, the line, and the emotional association that goes with them. The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding is the argument for keeping that identity narrow, and Building Strong Brands is the argument for measuring what it is worth.

Service Branding:

Service branding does the same work for something intangible, where there is no packaging to carry the identity and the experience itself has to do it. That is where Building a StoryBrand 2.0 earns its place, because a service brand mostly lives or dies on whether its message is clear.

Corporate Branding:

Corporate branding builds the identity of the whole company, which then vouches for everything under it. This is the territory of Designing Brand Identity, the only book here written for a whole team rather than one reader, and it is the mode most of our own brand strategy work sits in.

Place Branding:

Place branding applies the same tools to a city, a state or a country, using name, story and symbol to attract visitors, residents or investment. No book on this list is dedicated to it. If that is your problem, treat The Brand Gap as the framework and go and find case studies elsewhere.

Co-branding:

Co-branding pairs two brands on a single offer so that each lends the other something it does not have: reach, credibility, or a different customer. Aaker's work on brand equity is the useful lens here, because co-branding is a transaction in borrowed equity and the borrower usually gets the worse deal.

No-brand branding:

No-brand branding deliberately strips out the usual signals, such as the ownable name and the elaborate mark, and lets the product, the price and the packaging carry the identity instead. It is not the absence of branding. It is a branding decision that is expensive to get right, which is why so few companies sustain it.

Relevance of Branding for Businesses

Nine statistics turn up on almost every page written to answer this question: that 64% of customers trust brands sharing their values, that consistent branding lifts revenue by 33%, and seven more like them. All nine trace to a single aggregator page. I went looking for the underlying studies on 30 July 2026 and could not reach a primary source for any of them. The aggregator's own reference list does not cover the figures it is credited with. So you will not find them quoted here, and I am not substituting different numbers of the same quality.

That is not a claim that branding does not pay. It is a claim that the industry has been recycling the same undated percentages for over a decade, and a page recommending books about evidence should not be built on numbers nobody can trace. If you want the serious version of the argument, it is in a book rather than in a statistic: David Aaker's Building Strong Brands is the work that made brand equity something a finance department will discuss, and it is on this list for that reason rather than for its readability.

Top 12 Branding Books That You Must Read! 

Twelve books, each with the title and author as the publisher has them, and each linked to an edition you can actually buy.

Start with Why: How Great Leaders Inspire Everyone To Take Action

By- Simon Sinek

Edition: Penguin's 15th Anniversary Edition, published 15 May 2025 with a new foreword, updated examples and revised stories. The 2011 Portfolio paperback that most reading lists point at is still in print and still correct, but it is no longer the current edition and is not stocked on Amazon India under that ISBN.

The book is about purpose: why you do what you do, before what you do or how. Sinek's question is why some people and organisations are more innovative and more influential than others, and his answer is that they communicate from the inside out. He builds it from Apple, the Wright brothers and Martin Luther King Jr.

Be clear about what this is. It is a book about internal alignment and leadership communication that has been widely adopted as a branding book. It will help you write a positioning line you believe. It will not help you build an identity system.

The Brand Gap: How To Bridge The Distance Between Business Strategy And Design

By- Marty Neumeier

Edition: the link above is the revised edition, New Riders, 25 August 2005, which is in stock. The 2003 first edition, still the one most lists point at, is marked unavailable on Amazon India with no restock date.

Neumeier's subject is the distance between the people who decide strategy and the people who make things, and how brands fall into it. He organises the answer around five disciplines: differentiation, collaboration, innovation, validation and cultivation.

It is short, set like a presentation, and readable in an evening. If somebody on your team needs to understand what branding is before they can be useful in a project, this is the one to hand them first.

The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding: How to Build a Product or Service into a World-Class Brand

By- Al Ries and Laura Ries

Format note: the heading links the Kindle edition, dated 6 October 2009, because that is the version reliably in stock in India. The HarperBusiness paperback is dated 17 September 2002 and is usually available only from third-party sellers.

The core idea is positioning: that a brand is a word you own in somebody's head, and that almost everything companies do dilutes that word. The twenty-two laws are the consequences, given plain rule names such as the Law of Expansion, the Law of Contraction, the Law of Consistency and the Law of Mortality. The first pair is where most readers argue back, because narrowing a brand is the advice nobody with a growth target wants.

Al Ries died on 7 October 2022. Read the laws as a strong argument rather than as physics, and it holds up well. Read them as literal rules and you will end up defending positions the market stopped rewarding some time ago.

Personal Branding For Dummies

By- Susan Chritton

Edition: the link above is the second edition, Wiley, 14 July 2014, ISBN 978-1118915554, which adds material on social platforms and online communities. The 2012 first edition is the one usually cited.

Chritton, a personal brand strategist and career counsellor, treats personal branding as a process with steps rather than as a personality trait: work out what you are actually known for, decide what you want to be known for, close the gap deliberately, and maintain it.

The For Dummies format is the point. If you want a book that tells you what to do on Tuesday, this is it. If you want an argument, read Peters instead.

The Power of Visual Storytelling

By- Ekaterina Walter & Jessica Gioglio

Edition note: McGraw-Hill, 3 March 2014, 221 pages. There is no revised edition. The full subtitle is How to Use Visuals, Videos, and Social Media to Market Your Brand.

Walter and Gioglio make the case for building a brand out of images and video rather than out of copy, and they work through how the platforms of the time rewarded it.

I am keeping it on the list, and I am telling you plainly that it is the most dated book here. It was written before short-form vertical video reorganised social entirely. The principles about visual consistency survive that. The platform tactics do not, and you should read the second half as history.

The Brand You50

By- Tom Peters

Title: the book is The Brand You50, not The Brand You 50, which is how it is usually typed. Knopf, 21 September 1999, part of the Reinventing Work series. Its full subtitle is Fifty Ways to Transform Yourself from an "Employee" into a Brand That Shouts Distinction, Commitment, and Passion!

Peters wrote this at the peak of the argument that job security was over and the only durable asset was your own reputation. Fifty short provocations, formatted to be shouted.

Twenty-seven years on, its assumptions about work have become the ordinary background of freelance and portfolio careers, which makes parts of it read as obvious and other parts as period writing. Read it for the energy, not for the tactics.

Building a StoryBrand 2.0: Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen

By- Donald Miller

Edition: HarperCollins Leadership published Building a StoryBrand 2.0 on 7 January 2025 as a fully revised and updated replacement for the 2017 edition, which is what most reading lists still link. Buy 2.0.

Miller's argument is that most companies cast themselves as the hero of their own story, and that this is exactly backwards. The customer is the hero. You are the guide. Everything you write should follow from that reversal.

Of everything on this list, this is the book whose output you can see fastest, because it ends in sentences you can put on a home page. It is also the pick this list shares with our reading list for web and graphic designers, which is otherwise a completely separate set of thirteen books aimed at people who make things rather than at people who position them. If you build as well as strategise, read both lists.

Designing Brand Identity: A Comprehensive Guide to the World of Brands and Branding

By- Alina Wheeler & Rob Meyerson

Edition and authorship: the sixth edition, Wiley, 2024, ISBN 978-1119984818, is co-authored by Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson and carries a new subtitle. The fifth edition of 2017, subtitled An Essential Guide for the Whole Branding Team, is the one most lists still cite. Alina Wheeler died in 2023; Meyerson carried the book forward.

Wheeler's contribution was to break branding into a repeatable five-phase methodology and lay out every topic on a single spread, so that a strategist, a designer and a client can all read the same page and mean the same thing. The book has been published in eleven languages.

This is the reference volume, built to be opened at the relevant spread rather than read straight through, and it is the closest thing on this list to a manual for running the branding process end to end.

Sticky Branding: 12.5 Principles to Stand Out, Attract Customers & Grow an Incredible Brand

By- Jeremy Miller

No link, and a title note. The subtitle ends "Grow an Incredible Brand", not "Grown", which is how it is usually reproduced. The Amazon India listing for the Dundurn paperback, published 10 January 2015, reads "currently unavailable" with no restock date, so there is nothing worth linking. The book is still sold through other Amazon storefronts and through used-book retailers; title and author are enough to find it.

Miller's subject is the mid-size company in a category where every competitor sounds the same, and his principles are about becoming the firm that gets called first. It leans heavily on interviews with owner-operated businesses.

It is the most operational book here for an agency or a professional services firm, which is a narrow audience but an underserved one.

Identity Designed: The Definitive Guide to Visual Branding

By- David Airey

Edition, plus a companion: Rockport, 2019, 288 pages, in stock in India. Airey also published Identity Designed: The Process with Rockport in 2024, organised around research, strategy, design and implementation.

The 2019 book is built from sixteen identity projects taken apart in detail: what the client asked for, what the studio found, what got made and why. It is the closest thing in print to sitting in on somebody else's project.

Read it alongside our own account of the logo design process if you want to compare how a studio describes the work to how it is actually sequenced.

Building Strong Brands

By- David A. Aaker

Edition: Free Press, 1 December 1995, ISBN 978-0029001516, in stock in India. Published in 1995 and never revised.

Aaker's contribution is brand equity: the argument that a brand is an asset with components you can name, track and put a number against, rather than a feeling the marketing department has. Brand identity, brand associations, perceived quality, loyalty.

It is the most academic book on this list and the one that carries the most weight in a room full of people who control budget. Every later book here is downstream of it, including the ones that never cite it.

Logo Design Love: A Guide to Creating Iconic Brand Marks

By- David Airey

Author, title and edition. The author is David Airey, frequently misspelled "David Alrey", and it is the same David Airey who wrote Identity Designed two entries above. A third edition appeared in 2026 from New Riders, 224 pages, subtitled A Guide to Creating Iconic Brand Marks. It supersedes the 2014 second edition, which is the one most lists still cite, under the old subtitle.

Airey walks through how a mark gets made: the questions asked before anything is drawn, the sketching, the presentation to a client, and the reasons good marks get rejected.

Note what it is not. This is a craft book about one deliverable. If you are trying to work out what the brand should stand for, none of it will help, and you want Neumeier or Wheeler instead.

Questions I get asked about branding books

If I only read one, which one?

The Brand Gap, revised edition. It is 208 pages, set like a slide deck, readable in an evening, and it gives you the vocabulary to read every other book here. If you already have that vocabulary, go to Designing Brand Identity instead, because it is the only one on this list written for a team rather than for one reader.

Does the edition really matter for a branding book?

Sometimes badly. Building a StoryBrand 2.0 is a full revision, not a reprint with a new cover. Designing Brand Identity gained a co-author and a new subtitle between the fifth and sixth editions. On the other hand Building Strong Brands has not been revised since 1995 and does not need to be. The rule is not "always buy the newest". It is "know which one you are holding".

Are these books useful in India, or is it all American case studies?

Mostly American and European case studies, yes. The frameworks travel. The examples do not, and you should expect to translate them. That is one reason we write up our own work from Kolkata rather than only recommending other people's books.

Why is there no book here about AI and branding?

The gap is deliberate. The field is moving faster than book publishing can, and the material worth reading right now sits in primary documentation and in practitioners' own write-ups rather than between covers. Adding a title to twelve just to look current is how lists like this go stale in the first place. When a book earns the place, this list will change.

Is this list the same as your list for designers?

No. Our 13 must-read books for web and graphic designers is aimed at people making the work: typography, grids, colour, design systems, front-end. This list is aimed at people deciding what the brand should be. Exactly one book appears on both, Building a StoryBrand, because messaging is the one job that sits on the line between the two. Nothing else is shared.

How I would use this list

Do not read twelve branding books. Read the one that matches the problem in front of you, apply it badly, and then read the next one. A reading list is not an education, and a person who has read all twelve of these and shipped nothing is less useful to a client than a person who has read Neumeier and rebuilt one home page properly.

What I would ask of any list like this, including this one, is simple: check whether the person who wrote it went back. Six of these twelve books have a newer edition than the one a typical reading list points at, and two are no longer buyable on Amazon India at all. If you are planning a rebrand, apply the same test to every source you are relying on.
